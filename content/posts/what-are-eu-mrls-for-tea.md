# What Are EU MRLs for Tea? A Simple Buyer's Guide

If you buy or import tea into the European Union, you will hear the term "MRL" very quickly. Sellers mention it, labs test against it, and customs can hold a shipment over it. Yet most people using the term could not explain what it means. That gap causes real confusion — and real rejected containers.

This guide explains EU maximum residue levels (MRLs) for tea in plain language. You will learn what an MRL is, where the rules live, why the famous 0.01 mg/kg figure is so important, and how to check the limit for any pesticide yourself.

## What an MRL actually is

MRL stands for **maximum residue level**. It is the highest amount of a pesticide residue that is legally allowed in or on a product.

An MRL is a *legal compliance limit*, not a statement of safety and not a target for growers. Think of it like a speed limit. Driving at 49 mph in a 50 mph zone is legal, but that does not make 49 mph the "ideal" or "safe" speed — it is simply within the law. A residue below the MRL is compliant; a residue above it is not.

Two ideas follow from this, and both matter for tea:

- MRLs are set **per pesticide and per product**. The limit for one chemical on tea can be completely different from the limit for another chemical on tea, or for the same chemical on another crop.
- A result below an MRL does not mean "no pesticide was used." It means the residue is within the legal limit.

## The rulebook: Regulation (EC) No 396/2005

The main EU law is **Regulation (EC) No 396/2005** on maximum residue levels of pesticides in or on food and feed of plant and animal origin. It applies across the EU and is the reference point for nearly every pesticide residue question about imported tea.

Under Article 18(1) of that regulation, products listed in Annex I must not contain pesticide residues above:

1. the specific MRLs set out in **Annexes II and III**; or
2. where no specific MRL exists, the default value.

That second point is where most of the misunderstanding begins.

## The 0.01 mg/kg default limit

Article 18(1) sets a general default of **0.01 mg/kg**. In the regulation's exact wording, this applies to products "for which no specific MRL is set out in Annexes II or III, or for active substances not listed in Annex IV" (unless a different default is fixed under Article 45(2); such default values are listed in Annex V).

In practice, if there is no specific MRL for a given pesticide on tea, the default of 0.01 mg/kg applies. For a buyer, this is the single most useful number to remember.

### What the default is not

The 0.01 mg/kg figure is widely misreported. Let us clear up three common errors.

- **It is not "zero residue."** A product can comply while containing a measurable residue below the limit. The number is a legal ceiling, not a ban on all residue.
- **It is not a safety threshold.** It is a compliance limit set broadly at the level of quantification — the point at which labs can reliably measure a substance. Being below it is a legal status, not a medical judgement.
- **It does not replace specific MRLs.** Where a specific MRL exists for a pesticide on tea, that specific value is the one that applies, not the default.

If you take only one thing from this guide, make it this: check whether a specific MRL exists first. Only fall back to the default when none does.

## How tea is classified in the regulation

EU rules are precise about what counts as "tea." In Annex I of Regulation 396/2005, tea appears under category 06 (teas, coffee, herbal infusions, cocoa and carobs) with the commodity code **0610000**. The description covers "tea (dried leaves and stalks, fermented or otherwise, of *Camellia sinensis*)".

This precision matters. Herbal infusions (tisanes) are a different commodity from *Camellia sinensis* tea, and the MRLs can differ. If you import a blended product, check which commodity code actually applies to each part of it. Assuming "it is all tea" is a common and avoidable mistake.

Specific MRLs exist for many active substances on tea. Because they are set substance by substance, there is no single "tea MRL" number to memorise.

## How to look up the MRL for a specific pesticide

You do not need a lawyer to check a limit. The EU publishes an official search tool, the **EU Pesticides Database**.

Here is a practical approach:

1. Open the EU Pesticides Database MRL search (linked in Sources below).
2. Select the commodity "tea" (code 0610000) or type it into the product search.
3. Search for the active substance you are interested in.
4. Read the applicable MRL and note any conditions or footnotes attached to it.
5. If no specific MRL appears for that substance on tea, the 0.01 mg/kg default is the value to work to.

A useful habit: save a screenshot or PDF of the result with the date you checked it. Because the database is an interactive application, it is best to record what you saw rather than rely on a second-hand number.

### Why you should not trust copied numbers

You will find MRL tables circulating in emails and spreadsheets. Treat them as leads, not authority. Article 18 refers to Annexes II and III, and these annexes are amended frequently as scientific reviews and policy decisions change. A number that was correct last year may have been revised. Always confirm against the current consolidated version of the regulation or the official database before you rely on it.

## Processing, dilution and the ban on "fixing" a failing lot

A question that comes up in trade is whether a non-compliant tea can be blended down with clean tea to bring the residue below the limit. Regulation 396/2005 is clear: Article 19 prohibits processing or diluting non-compliant products to bring them into compliance.

For a buyer, this is more than a legal detail. If a supplier offers to "blend away" a residue problem instead of explaining and correcting it, that is a warning sign. The compliant path is to identify the source, correct the production or supply issue, and verify with testing — not to mix the problem away.

## What this means for you in practice

Whether you are a small retailer or a first-time importer, a few habits will keep you out of trouble:

- **Ask which substances were tested**, not just "is it compliant?" A test that covered ten pesticides says nothing about the hundreds of others.
- **Ask for the limit used for each result**, so you can compare against the MRL that actually applies.
- **Check the current official text** for anything you are unsure about, and keep a dated record.
- **Confirm the commodity code** for blended or herbal products before assuming the tea MRL applies.

None of this requires specialist training. It requires slowing down and asking precise questions.

## Bottom line

EU MRLs for tea are legal limits on pesticide residues set under Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. Where a specific MRL exists for a pesticide on tea, use that value. Where none exists, the default of 0.01 mg/kg applies — but that figure is a compliance ceiling at the quantification level, not "zero residue" and not a safety threshold. Tea falls under commodity code 0610000, and you can look up any substance in the free EU Pesticides Database. Check the current version, keep a dated record, and never accept dilution as a fix for a failing lot.
