## 1. EU — Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (pesticide MRLs)
- Full title: Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 on maximum residue levels of pesticides in or on food and feed of plant and animal origin.
- Official consolidated text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2005/396/oj/eng
- Article 18(1): products in Annex I must not contain pesticide residues exceeding:
  (a) the MRLs set in Annexes II and III; or
  (b) **0.01 mg/kg** for products with no specific MRL in Annexes II/III, or for active substances not listed in Annex IV (unless different default values are fixed under Art.45(2)). Such default values are listed in Annex V.
  - EXACT wording: "0,01 mg/kg for those products for which no specific MRL is set out in Annexes II or III, or for active substances not listed in Annex IV..."
  - This 0.01 mg/kg is the **default MRL** — NOT a blanket "zero residue" rule, NOT a safety threshold. It is a legal compliance limit at the LOQ level.
- Chapter III (Art.18/19): Art.19 prohibits processing/diluting non-compliant products to bring them into compliance.
- Tea: "tea (dried leaves and stalks, fermented or otherwise, of Camellia sinensis)" is a commodity under Annex I, category 06 (teas, coffee, herbal infusions...), code 0610000. Specific MRLs exist for many active substances on tea — the applicable value must be looked up per substance.
- Official lookup tool: EU Pesticides Database (MRLs search): https://ec.europa.eu/food/plant/pesticides/eu-pesticides-database/start/screen/mrls
  - The database is a JS app; exact per-substance MRL values cannot be scraped as plain text. Therefore articles must NOT quote specific tea MRL numbers unless separately verified. Instead, teach readers HOW to look them up.
- Regulation is amended frequently (Annexes II/III/IV/V). Always check the current consolidated version.

## 2. EU — contaminants (heavy metals, mycotoxins) — Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006
- Title: Commission Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006 setting maximum levels for certain contaminants in foodstuffs.
- Official summary: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/LSU/?uri=CELEX:32006R1881
- Scope includes: mycotoxins (aflatoxins, ochratoxin A, Fusarium toxins, patulin, citrinin, ergot alkaloids); metals (lead, cadmium, mercury, inorganic tin, arsenic); 3-MCPD; dioxins/PCBs; PAHs; melamine; nitrates; perchlorate, etc.
- Key principle: food above the annex limits may not be sold; limits apply to the edible part and to compound/processed/dried/diluted foods; **ban on mixing** compliant with non-compliant food.
- IMPORTANT — do NOT invent a tea-specific lead limit:
  - The lead maximum levels were updated by Commission Regulation (EU) 2021/1317 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=uriserv:OJ.L_.2021.286.01.0001.01.ENG), which replaced subsection 3.1 (Lead). The listed foods include milk, infant formula, cereals, vegetables, fruit, fish, spices, honey, salt, etc. **"Tea" is NOT listed as a separate commodity with its own lead maximum level in that list.** Dried spices DO have lead limits (e.g. fruit spices 0.60, root/rhizome 1.50, bark 2.0, bud/flower pistil 1.0, seed spices 0.90 mg/kg) — but tea is not "dried spices" in this regulation.
  - SAFE ARTICLE LANGUAGE: "EU law sets contaminant maximum levels in Regulation 1881/2006, but it does not set a single dedicated EU-wide lead limit specifically for tea leaves. Buyers therefore rely on import controls, national enforcement and their own COA testing." Do NOT state a numeric EU lead limit for tea.
- Analytical performance rules: Commission Regulation (EC) No 333/2007 (sampling and analysis methods for trace elements/contaminants). Measurement uncertainty: results reported as x +/- U with coverage factor 2 (~95% confidence). Lot accepted if result does not exceed ML taking uncertainty & recovery into account; rejected if it exceeds beyond reasonable doubt. Official: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02007R0333-20191214
- Mycotoxins in tea: Regulation 1881/2006 sets ochratoxin A limits for wine, coffee, dried fruit, spices, etc. There is NO dedicated OTA limit specifically for tea in that annex. Do NOT invent one.

## 3. USA — import requirements
### 3a. Prior Notice (21 CFR Part 1 Subpart I)
- Official eCFR: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-I
- Applies to all food for humans/animals imported or offered for import (incl. gifts, samples, transshipments, goods for foreign trade zones). § 1.277(a).
- Exemptions § 1.277(b): personal food carried with traveler; non-commercial personal gifts; food imported then exported without leaving port; USDA-exclusive meat/poultry/egg; diplomatic mail.
- Must be submitted ELECTRONICALLY to FDA (§ 1.280): via ABI/ACE/ITDS or FDA PNSI (https://www.access.fda.gov/). Information in English (names/street names may be foreign language but in Latin/Roman alphabet).
- TIMING (§ 1.279): prior notice must be submitted and confirmed by FDA for review no less than:
  - **2 hours** before arrival — road
  - **4 hours** before arrival — rail or air
  - **8 hours** before arrival — water (vessel)
  - International mail: before the article is sent to the US (§ 1.279(c)).
  - Earliest: no more than **30 calendar days** before anticipated arrival via ABI/ACE/ITDS; no more than **15 calendar days** via FDA PNSI (§ 1.279(b)).
- Content § 1.281: submitter/transmitter info, entry type, FDA product code, common/usual name, quantity, lot/code numbers, manufacturer or grower info, FDA country of production, shipper, country of shipment, port of arrival/date/time, importer, owner, ultimate consignee, mode of transport, carrier code, shipment info (bill of lading/airway bill/vessel), and whether the food was previously refused by any country.
- Consequences § 1.283: no / inaccurate / late prior notice → refusal of admission under section 801(m)(1) of the FD&C Act. Re-submission within 10 calendar days of refusal notice; FDA review request within 5 calendar days. International mail held by CBP up to 72 hours.
- Note: § 1.285 concerns food from UNREGISTERED facilities — hold under 801(l); valid registration number must be submitted within 30 calendar days. This is a different issue from prior notice timing.

### 3b. FSVP — Foreign Supplier Verification Programs (21 CFR Part 1 Subpart L)
- Official eCFR: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-L
- Statutory basis: section 805 of the FD&C Act (21 U.S.C. 384a).
- "Importer" = US owner or consignee at time of entry; if none, the US agent/representative of the foreign owner/consignee (with signed consent). § 1.500.
- Core duty § 1.502(a): for each food imported, develop/maintain/follow an FSVP giving adequate assurance the foreign supplier produces food using processes providing at least the same level of public health protection as US requirements (FSMA §418/419 where applicable) and complies with §§402 and 403(w).
- Must be developed & performed by a "qualified individual" (education/training/experience, able to read records); onsite audits by a "qualified auditor". § 1.503.
- HAZARD ANALYSIS § 1.504: written (regardless of outcome); identify/evaluate known or reasonably foreseeable hazards: biological (parasites, pathogens), chemical (incl. **pesticide and drug residues**, natural toxins, unapproved additives, allergens), physical (stones, glass, metal). Must evaluate probability & severity.
- SUPPLIER EVALUATION & APPROVAL § 1.505: consider hazard analysis; who controls the hazard; supplier performance incl. procedures, applicable FDA regs, whether supplier is subject to a **warning letter / import alert / other FDA compliance action**, and food safety history (testing results, audits, responsiveness). Approve and document; reevaluate with new info and at least every **3 years**.
- VERIFICATION ACTIVITIES § 1.506: establish written procedures; determine & document which activities and frequency. Options: (A) onsite audits, (B) **sampling and testing**, (C) review of supplier food safety records, (D) other appropriate activities. For a hazard controlled by the supplier with reasonable probability of serious adverse health consequences/death: onsite audit before initial import and at least annually unless adequately justified otherwise.
  - § 1.506(e)(2)(ii): may NOT rely on the foreign supplier itself (or its employees) to perform verification activities, EXCEPT sampling & testing.
  - Sampling/testing records must include lot number, samples tested, tests & analytical methods, dates, results, corrective actions, lab identification, and that testing was by a qualified individual. § 1.506(e)(1)(ii).
  - No financial conflicts of interest; payment not tied to results. § 1.506(e)(4).
- CORRECTIVE ACTIONS § 1.508: if supplier not compliant, take prompt appropriate action (may include discontinuing the supplier).
- ENTRY IDENTIFICATION § 1.509(a): for each line entry, provide importer's name, email address, and FDA-recognized **unique facility identifier (UFI)** electronically to CBP.
- RECORDKEEPING § 1.510: retain records at least **2 years** after creation/obtainment; process/procedure records at least 2 years after use discontinued.
- Exemptions: § 1.501 (juice/fish under HACCP, research/evaluation food, personal consumption, alcoholic beverages, transshipment, goods for processing & re-export, US-returned goods, USDA meat/poultry/egg). § 1.511 (certain dietary supplements). § 1.512 (very small importer — human food averaging **less than $1 million/year** over prior 3 years; reduced requirements but still §§1.502, 1.503, 1.509). § 1.513 (countries with recognized equivalent systems).

### 3c. Import Alerts / Detention Without Physical Examination (DWPE)
- FDA Import Alerts are enforcement tools to prevent violative products from entering the US. An "alert" lists firms/products subject to DWPE.
- DWPE means the product may be detained at the border WITHOUT physical examination — the importer must provide evidence to overcome the appearance of a violation.
- To be removed from an import alert, the firm must provide evidence to FDA adequately demonstrating it resolved the conditions that gave rise to the violation. Guidance: FDA Regulatory Procedures Manual (RPM) Chapter 9-8 "Detention without Physical Examination (DWPE)".
- "Red list" = firms currently subject to DWPE; "Green list" = firms that have met conditions for removal. (Standard FDA terminology.)
- Relevant alert numbers (verified):
  - **Import Alert 99-41** — DWPE based on FSVP violations (cited in FDA warning letter re FSVP, e.g. Daisy Global Trading Co, 2021).
  - **Import Alert 99-48** — "Detention without Physical Examination of Foods Due to Chemical Contamination" — broad range of human-made chemicals incl. benzene, dioxins, PCBs, PFAS. (FDA Constituent Update, 2024.)
- Official Import Alert list: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cms_ia/ialist.html
- IMPORTANT — do NOT claim there is a "tea-specific" FDA import alert. Tea is subject to general food/chemical/FSVP alerts. If a specific alert is referenced, verify its number first.

### 3d. FDA food labeling / health claims
- A product is a "drug" under FD&C Act §201(g)(1) (21 U.S.C. 321(g)(1)) if intended for use in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease, or to affect the structure or any function of the body. Claims that establish this intended use can make an unapproved product an illegal drug. (Basis of FDA warning letters.)
- Ordinary tea sold as a food may NOT bear disease-treatment/health claims. This blog itself must contain ZERO health/cure claims (per user's hard rule).
- FDA Nutrition Facts label rules (2016 update) apply to packaged foods; (see fda.gov nutrition label page).

### 3e. FDA registration & facility
- Food facilities (US and foreign) that manufacture/process/pack/hold food for US consumption must register with FDA (FD&C Act §415). Prior notice alone is not enough; unregistered-facility food can be held under §1.285.

## 4. Testing / labs / documentation
- Third-party labs commonly used for tea: Eurofins, SGS, Intertek, Bureau Veritas, ALS, NSF. (These are real, well-known testing companies — safe to name as examples. Do NOT claim any specific lab certifies a particular brand.)
- ISO/IEC 17025 is the international standard for the competence of testing and calibration laboratories. Labs accredited to it are technically competent. (Real, safe.)
- A Certificate of Analysis (COA) / test report should identify: the laboratory (name, accreditation), sample/lot ID, date sampled & date reported, analytes tested, method used, result & unit, LOQ/LOD, and pass/fail vs a stated limit.
- LOD (limit of detection) = lowest amount detectable; LOQ (limit of quantification) = lowest amount quantifiable with acceptable precision/accuracy. "Not detected" (ND) does not mean "zero" — it means below the method's detection capability.
- Sampling is statistical: testing one bag from a lot does not prove every bag is compliant. Refer to ISO 2859 / general sampling principles conceptually (do not invent a specific sampling plan number as law).

## 5. GB standard (China)
- GB 2763 is China's national standard "Maximum residue limits for pesticides in food" (食品安全国家标准 食品中农药最大残留限量). It sets MRLs for pesticides across foods including tea.
- It is updated periodically (e.g. GB 2763-2021 and later revisions). Because version numbers and specific limits change and could not be verified from an official primary source in this session, articles must:
  - Describe GB 2763 conceptually as China's pesticide MRL standard covering tea.
  - NOT quote specific GB numeric limits for tea.
  - NOT assert a definitive "GB is always higher/looser than EU" — instead explain that limits differ by substance and that EU applies the 0.01 mg/kg default where no specific MRL exists, which is often stricter than GB for substances with a GB-specific MRL. Frame as general tendency, not an absolute rule.

## 6. Australia / Canada (overview — keep high level, no invented numbers)
- Canada: Health Canada sets pesticide MRLs under the Pest Control Products Act; CFIA enforces. Food imported must comply with Canadian MRLs. Keep general.
- Australia/New Zealand: FSANZ (Food Standards Australia New Zealand) sets maximum residue limits in the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code (Schedule 18 / S18). Keep general.
- Do NOT quote specific AU/CA numeric MRLs for tea.

## 7. Storage / mycotoxins / processing (conceptual, no invented thresholds)
- Tea is hygroscopic (absorbs moisture/odors). High humidity + poor storage → mold growth risk → potential mycotoxin contamination (e.g. ochratoxin A produced by certain Aspergillus/Penicillium molds). Keep conceptual; no invented moisture % thresholds as law.
- Good storage: cool, dry, sealed, away from light/odors; first-in-first-out. Pu-erh aging requires controlled humidity — too dry = no aging, too wet = mold/rot. Keep conceptual.
- "Aging removes pesticides" — NO credible basis to claim aging reliably removes pesticide residues to safe levels; degradation depends on substance, time, conditions; do NOT reassure readers that aging makes a contaminated tea safe. This is a myth-debunking article (title 18).

## 8. "Zero pesticide residue" / "Organic" (claim discipline)
- "Zero pesticide residue" is a marketing phrase, not a regulatory category. Absence of detectable residue ≠ no chemicals were used; it depends on what was tested, the method's LOQ, sampling, and timing.
- Organic (EU / USDA NOP): restricts which substances may be used in production, but organic certification is a PROCESS standard, not a guarantee of zero residues; drift/contamination can still occur. Residue testing still matters. Do NOT claim organic = residue-free.
